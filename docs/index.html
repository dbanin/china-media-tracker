Chinese state media, traced through the world's newsrooms.

This instrument looks for two kinds of article in every country it monitors: text placed by the Chinese state and published unaltered (state origin), and local reporting that carries claims from Chinese officials or state media without independent checking (unchecked state sourcing). Ordinary China coverage is only the denominator. Counts come from the full text of articles in a fixed registry of outlets, every day. Hatched countries are not monitored. That is absence of data, not absence of content.

      Methodology

      State origin. The text was written by an entity of the Chinese state and published essentially unaltered. Xinhua wire copy, CGTN promotional releases, China Daily and Global Times syndication, sponsored or branded placements paid for by Chinese state entities, and signed opinion pieces by Chinese ambassadors and embassy officials. The defining property is that nobody at the receiving publication formed an editorial view about the content.

      Unchecked state sourcing. Called unverified relay in the codebook, the model prompt and the data files. The article was written by the local outlet, but it passes on official Chinese sourcing without independent confirmation. A ministry spokesperson is quoted and nothing checks the claim. Xinhua is cited as the authority for a factual assertion with no second source. State media is described as reporting something, and the report is treated as the fact.

      Independent journalism. The outlet's own reporting, including reporting that quotes Chinese officials but confirms, contextualizes or contests what they say.

      Not relevant. Does not concern China.

      Unchecked state sourcing: provisional, then published or withheld. Until the model stage has run, unchecked state sourcing is shown as not yet measured, never as zero. Once it has run and before any reliability study, the counts are shown as provisional: they are one model's judgement, nothing has checked them, and every figure that contains them says so, on the map legend, in the tooltip and panel, and as relay_status in the CSV export. The study runs by itself as soon as the monthly model budget can pay for it: the same model re-judges a stratified sample of articles it has already labelled, seeing exactly what it saw the first time. If Cohen's kappa on the unchecked sourcing versus independent journalism judgement is at or above the threshold, the provisional marking is replaced by a statement of what the study measured; if it is below, the counts are withheld, not merely annotated, and they stay withheld for any language whose own kappa falls below it. Provisional means not checked yet, never checked and failed. A same model rerun measures whether the judgement is stable, not whether it is correct, and no article has been read by a person, so a published unchecked state sourcing count carries that qualifier wherever it appears and should be read as consistent rather than verified. That boundary is where classification error concentrates. State-linked articles add the candidates still waiting for the judgement, so that measure mixes a measured quantity with an unmeasured one.

      Routes and arrival. Every state origin label records its route, the mechanism named by the signature that established it: a wire credit line or dateline, a press release distribution stamp, a sponsored or partner disclosure, or a byline by a Chinese diplomat. A label the model gave without a signature is unattributed. Every article also records where it was collected: an editorial feed, or the outlet's press release, sponsored or partner section. The route filter shows state origin for one route or one arrival at a time.

      Color scale. Exactly zero is white. Any positive value takes one of seven tinted steps from pale pink to deep red. The step edges follow a square law between zero and a cap, the 95th percentile of every country's value on every date of the timeline for the chosen measure and window, so the scale does not move when the day changes and a shade means the same number on every date. Values above the cap sit in the darkest, overflow step, and the legend gives the highest value. Countries withheld, unreadable, not monitored or with too small a denominator have their own patterns and are never on the scale.

      Themes. Every article that concerns China is tagged with each theme whose terms appear in its headline, its feed summary or anywhere in its body. Until themes version 2026.09.4 only the first 800 characters of the body were read, which favored whatever an article names early. An article can carry several themes, so theme counts do not add up to article counts, and one with none is Other. The theme counter carries the same four category buttons as the map, and the two move together, so it can show themes for state origin, unchecked state sourcing, state-linked articles or all China coverage. It has its own window and timeline, separate from the map. The One country toggle, either Country search box, or a click on a country opens the single country view in both places, and Whole world returns to every country. Its tags are keyword matches, not a reading of the article, so they should be checked by hand before a theme count is cited.

      Denominators. Per outlet divides by the country's active monitored outlets. Share of monitored output divides by every item the country's monitored outlets put in their feeds during the window, whether or not it concerns China, and the tooltip gives the same figure per 1,000 published items; until audience ranks are recorded in the registry that is every active outlet, not the largest publications, and no share is shown for a country with fewer than five outlets, where it would describe a handful of feeds. Per capita divides by the resident population in sources/population.json and is not shown for territories under 100,000 residents; population measures people, not media saturation, so it is the least defensible of the three. Paywalled articles are never classified, so they are missing from every count, and from the share of China coverage, where an unread article is in neither half. They do stay in the share of monitored output denominator, which counts every item the monitored outlets published whether or not it could be read, so in a country with many paywalls that share is a lower bound. A country where paywalls pass a third of retrieved articles carries a warning on every figure shown for it.

      Placements and underlying items. One wire item syndicated to thirty outlets and thirty separate items are different phenomena. Near-duplicate titles within a country are linked as one underlying item, and the tooltip, the ranked list, the country panel and the CSV export show both numbers.

      Official Chinese sourcing, verification pending. Not a fifth category. A deterministic pass marks every article that cites Xinhua, CGTN, Global Times, China Daily, CCTV or People's Daily, quotes a foreign ministry spokesperson, a Chinese embassy or a named official spokesperson, or says that Chinese state media reported something. Those are the candidates for unchecked state sourcing. When more candidates wait than the daily model ceiling allows, the ones sent are a random draw with the same fraction in every country, recorded per day so counts can be reweighted.

      Gaps in collection. Feeds carry only their latest entries, so a skipped hourly run loses more items in a busy media market than in a quiet one; every poll records whether its window reached back to the previous one, and the estimated missed items are shown. Some outlets are collected from the owner's machine; days on which it ran too few hours are marked on the timeline and on the countries concerned, so a sleeping laptop is never read as a decline. Ruleset changes and the days still carrying labels from an older ruleset are marked on the timeline too, so a discontinuity is not read as a trend. The relevance gate carries its own version, marked on the timeline as well: it decides what enters the corpus rather than how an article is labelled, and because gate decisions are recorded per item and never revisited, a change to it applies only to items discovered after that date. Items the earlier gate rejected are not re-examined, and rejected items are pruned after three days.

      The critical distinction is between unchecked state sourcing and independent journalism, and it is a judgement about verification, not about topic or tone. An article highly critical of China that repeats an unverified Xinhua claim is still unchecked state sourcing. An article sympathetic to China that independently confirms every claim is independent journalism.

      Current notes

      What limits the figures today, written from the data at the last export.

        What this can tell you is how often, in a fixed set of outlets that I can actually read, Chinese state text appears unaltered, and how often local journalists pass on official Chinese claims without checking them. What it cannot tell you is what happens behind paywalls I do not cross, in outlets I do not track, in sections I have not searched, in languages I excluded, or in the ordinary gaps of an hourly poll of RSS feeds. A country that looks empty here may simply be a country I have not yet found a working feed for. Read the hatching before you read the color.
