        The Xinhua, CGTN and other state texts that outlets in the most countries ran. Pick one to see where and when it ran, and how much each outlet changed it where the copy was scored. Counted as everywhere on this site: each outlet once per text, a shared widget once per country, undetermined labels left out, release wires listed but never drawn.         Running a state text means an outlet published it under a state credit, a release stamp, a Chinese diplomat's byline or a disclosure, or as a close copy of credited state text.             Methodology

            State origin. The text was authored or commissioned by a Chinese state entity and published without substantive editorial alteration. Xinhua wire copy, CGTN promotional releases, China Daily and Global Times syndication, sponsored or branded placements commissioned by Chinese state entities, including those written to order by the publisher's own branded content studio, and signed opinion pieces by Chinese ambassadors and embassy officials. The defining property is that the words are the state's, written by it or to its order, and the receiving publication did not substantively alter them. Deciding to run a piece, or placing it on an opinion page, is not an alteration.

            Unchecked state sourcing. Called unverified relay in the codebook, the model prompt and the data files. The article was written by the local outlet, and its central content is a factual claim attributed to a Chinese official or state source, passed on without independent confirmation. A factual claim is an assertion of fact about events, numbers or actions: what happened, how many, who did what. A ministry spokesperson or Xinhua is the only authority for such a fact and the article treats it as established, with no second source. A stated position, policy, warning, threat, opinion or denial is not a factual claim: an article that reports what Beijing said it wants, intends, condemns or rejects is reporting the statement itself, and that is independent journalism even when no outside voice comments on it. "Exports rose 8.7 percent in September, China's commerce ministry said", reported as the figure with no other source, is unchecked state sourcing; "China's foreign ministry warned Manila it would take resolute measures to defend its sovereignty", reported without outside comment, is independent journalism. This factual-claim rule entered the model's instructions on 2026-10-12; model labels made before it are judged again in priority tiers, oldest first within each tier (the methodology file gives the order of the tiers), with up to half of each day's model calls, and each keeps its old label until the new one arrives.

            Independent journalism. Not state origin and not unchecked state sourcing. It includes the outlet's own reporting; copy from news agencies that are not Chinese state entities, such as Reuters or AP; reporting of a position, policy, warning, threat, opinion or denial attributed to a Chinese official or state source, with or without outside comment; reporting that quotes Chinese officials and confirms, contextualizes or contests what they say; and a press release from an issuer that is not a Chinese state entity, when it substantively concerns China.

            Not relevant. Does not concern China.

            Unchecked state sourcing: provisional, then published or withheld. Until the model stage has run, unchecked state sourcing is shown as not yet measured, never as zero. Once it has run and until hand coding has checked it, the counts are shown as provisional: they are one model's judgement, no person has checked them, and every figure that contains them says so, on the map legend, in the tooltip and panel, and as relay_status in the CSV export. The check is a hand coding study. The owner and two research assistants code, blind to the model's label, a fixed random sample: about 200 articles in each language with at least 300 model labelled articles, stratified by language and, within each language, by the model's current category, so that unchecked state sourcing is present in numbers that can test it. If Cohen's kappa between their codes and the model on the unchecked sourcing versus independent journalism judgement is at least 0.7, with the coders and the model each placing at least 10 articles in unchecked state sourcing, the provisional marking is removed; if it is below, the counts are withheld, not merely annotated, and they stay withheld for any language whose own kappa falls below it. Provisional means not checked yet, never checked and failed. A model re-judging its own sample, run by itself as the budget allows, is kept as a stability check: a same model rerun with the same prompt measures whether the judgement is stable, not whether it is correct, so it never removes the provisional marking, and a rerun below 0.7 withholds the counts. A rerun made under an earlier version of the classifier's instructions is kept only as history and neither supports nor withholds any count; a new one runs once enough articles are labelled under the current instructions. That boundary is where classification error concentrates. Candidates still waiting for that judgement are in no measure on the map, since adding them to judged counts would mix a measured quantity with an unmeasured one. The country panel and the CSV exports show them as their own figure, which is not a finding.

            Routes and arrival. Every state origin label records its route, the mechanism that shows the text is the state's: a wire credit line or dateline, a press release distribution stamp, a sponsored or partner disclosure, or a byline by a Chinese diplomat. A rules label takes its route from the signature that established it; a label the model gave takes it from the evidence the model names in the text, never from the signature that sent the article. A model label that names none is unattributed, or uncredited copy when the article was sent as a near copy of a state origin item another outlet ran with its credit line. An unattributed label rests on the model's judgement alone, and so does a sponsored disclosure the model named that the rules, which read the article itself and ignore sidebar and menu advertisements, did not find. The model is kept blind to outlet, country and address until after 23 October 2026, so such a call carries no marker a reader can check. An uncredited copy counts only when the item it repeats is settled state origin; a copy of an undetermined item repeats no known state original, so it is undetermined too. As in the original study, an item whose authorship or commissioning cannot be settled, and could be state, is undetermined and excluded from every category count: since 22 October 2026 these placements are in no count, share or denominator, China coverage included, and in no ranking, finding or map colour. They are shown only as their own figure, undetermined, in the summary table, the country panel, one line of What we found and the CSV exports, and the route filter's undetermined option draws them as a layer of their own, marked not counted. They never rejoin state origin; a blind hand check of a random sample of them reports what the coders find, as information only. Stored labels are unchanged. Every article also records where it was collected: an editorial feed, or the outlet's press release, sponsored or partner section. The route filter shows state origin for one route or one arrival at a time.

            Color scale. Exactly zero is white. Any positive value takes one of seven tinted steps from pale pink to deep red. The step edges follow a square law between zero and a cap, the 95th percentile of every country's value on every date of the timeline for the chosen measure and window (once the timeline passes 60 days, on 60 evenly spaced dates), so the scale does not move when the day changes and a shade means the same number on every date. The cap is not frozen for good: it is computed in the browser from the published data, and each data update, about once a day, adds the newest days and recomputes it over the timeline as it then stands, so a shade can stand for a different number from one week to the next. Every measure, basis and window has its own cap, so the key changes when any of them does, and its heading names all three. The edges are rounded to what the key prints, and the map colors with the rounded edges, so a printed tier is exactly the set of values that takes its shade: whole numbers for amounts, where a step too narrow to hold a whole number is left out rather than printed twice, and two significant figures for everything else. A share of monitored output is read per 1,000 published items, since it is a small fraction of one percent almost everywhere. Values above the cap sit in the darkest, overflow step, and the legend gives the highest value. The theme counter shades its cells by the number of articles in them, on the same steps and colors, with its own cap pooled over every day of its timeline for the chosen measure and window, recomputed at each data update in the same way. Countries withheld, unreadable, not monitored or with too small a denominator have their own patterns and are never on the scale.

            Stance toward government. A third grouping records whether each outlet supports its own government, is independent of it, or sides with the opposition, taken from a dated third-party source: Reporters Without Borders country pages, Freedom House country reports, the Media Ownership Monitor and Media Landscapes. An outlet's description of itself does not count. A stance is toward the government of the day, so each carries the year its source describes and a source from before a change of government, or older than 2023, is not used. State owned outlets without editorial independence are state controlled, from the sourced ownership. Outlets no such source describes are not assessed.

            Political leaning and ownership. An outlet is given a political leaning and an ownership type only where a cited source supports it: Wikipedia media lists with alignment columns, Media Bias/Fact Check, RSF's Media Ownership Monitor, the Reuters Institute Digital News Report, academic studies or the outlet's own editorial statement. Leaning is judged against each country's own political spectrum, so a centre right paper in one country and in another need not hold the same positions. Not assessed means no reliable source was found; it is never inferred from an outlet's name, language or country. A state owned outlet without editorial independence is shown as state controlled: its line is the government's, not a left or right one, and the ownership source stands for it. Many outlets are still not assessed, especially small ones, so the chart describes the assessed outlets, not every outlet. Ownership separates private outlets, domestic public service broadcasters with statutory editorial independence, state funded international broadcasters, state outlets without editorial independence, and party owned outlets. State funded international broadcasters are funded by a government to reach audiences abroad, with editorial independence set by statute, charter or grant agreement: Radio Free Asia and RFE/RL (grantees of the U.S. Agency for Global Media), Deutsche Welle, RFI and France 24, the BBC World Service language services, KBS World and RNZ Pacific. They are kept apart from domestic public service broadcasters such as the BBC at home, ABC, SBS or CBC, because their funder's foreign policy is part of their remit; outlets a state runs abroad without editorial independence, such as RT and Sputnik, stay under state. Media Bias/Fact Check is a US based rater that places outlets on a United States spectrum, so a leaning outside the United States that rests on it alone is kept but shown with a caution in the chart's table, and the note under the chart counts how many leanings come from each kind of source. The chart is all time, counts active outlets only, and leaves out the global press release distribution services, which are not national newsrooms. A share of articles partly counts outlets, because a leaning with more monitored outlets publishes more, so the table beside the chart also gives the rate per outlet.

            Foreign state ownership stakes. Where a foreign state holds a share of an outlet, or of the company that owns it, through a state media group, a state fund or a state owned enterprise, the registry records the stake with the holder, how it is tied to the state, the company it is held in, the share (or undisclosed), when it began and when it ended, each from a public source, and the source for its present status: the latest source still describing it, or the one dating its end. Nothing is inferred from an outlet's coverage, partners or content agreements, and a stake held through a private company with reported state ties is not recorded. Where sources agree that a stake exists but conflict on whether it still reaches the outlet, the record says disputed and names the sources on each side. The country panel shows each stake under the outlet's name with its source. An outlet with no stake shown has none recorded, which is not the same as having none: only stakes found in public reporting are listed. A stake records ownership; it is not a finding about editorial control. The first records are the 20 percent stake that a consortium of the state media company China International Television Corporation and the China-Africa Development Fund took in Independent News & Media South Africa, then the owner of IOL and the publisher of Isolezwe, in August 2013: disputed for IOL, which says it has been a separate company since 2014 and which amaBhungane reported moved to Sagarmatha Technologies, while Freedom House still called it Independent Media's digital version in 2022; and ended for Isolezwe when its title moved to another publisher at the end of July 2026.

            Themes. Every article that concerns China is tagged with each theme whose terms appear in its headline, its feed summary or anywhere in its body. Until themes version 2026.09.4 only the first 800 characters of the body were read, which favored whatever an article names early. An article can carry several themes, so theme counts do not add up to article counts, and one with none is Other. The theme counter carries the same three measure buttons as the map, and the two move together, so it can show themes for state origin, unchecked state sourcing or all China coverage. It has its own window and timeline, separate from the map. The One country toggle, either Country search box, or a click on a country opens the single country view in both places, and Whole world returns to every country. Its tags are keyword matches, not a reading of the article, so they should be checked by hand before a theme count is cited. Themes version 2026.10.1 adds three narrower themes: leaving the Belt and Road, Chinese overcapacity and dumping, and the EU and China trade war. They use proximity rules (a name of the Belt and Road near a word of leaving; anti-dumping near a word of Chinese origin; EU tariffs near electric vehicles) and exclusions, every term was read in its own language in the tracked outlets' coverage, and older articles are tagged for them newest first over several exports, so until that finishes their counts are partial. Themes version 2026.10.2 corrected their terms after every hit was read (no longer Volkswagen's own overcapacity or anti-dumping cases China opens; EU tariffs, quotas, safeguards and anti-dumping cases on Chinese goods now count), and older articles are re-tagged for it the same way. Under the leaning chart a table gives, per group of outlets, the share of China coverage carrying each of the three and the state origin share within it, with both denominators, marked provisional.

            Denominators. Per outlet we monitor divides by the country's active monitored outlets. % of all its news divides by every item the country's counted outlets put in their feeds during the window, whether or not it concerns China, and the tooltip gives the same figure per 1,000 published items. Where the registry records audience ranks for some of a country's outlets, the counted outlets are the ranked ones, at most the 30 best ranked; where it records none, they are every active outlet. The tooltip says which. No share is shown for a country with fewer than five outlets, where it would describe a handful of feeds. No per capita figure is shown, because population is a poor denominator for what outlets publish; each country's resident population is still given in the population column of the CSV of the current view, and no figure is divided by it. Paywalled articles are never classified, so they are missing from every count, and from the share of China coverage, where an unread article is in neither half. They do stay in the share of monitored output denominator, which counts every item the monitored outlets published whether or not it could be read, so in a country with many paywalls that share is a lower bound. A country where paywalls pass a third of retrieved articles carries a warning on every figure shown for it.

            Placements and underlying items. One wire item syndicated to thirty outlets and thirty separate items are different phenomena. Near-duplicate titles within a country are linked as one underlying item, and so, after the full text is fetched, are near-identical bodies in different outlets: a body link lowers a country's count of underlying items only when both placements are in that country, and a world count wherever they are. The tooltip, the ranked list, the country panel and the CSV export show both numbers. Counts are sensitive to how duplicates are defined: the rule here counts a release shown by one shared widget across a network of mastheads once per country, and counting each release one shared AAP widget showed across ten ACM mastheads that way, instead of once per masthead, cut Australia's state origin from 1,350 placements to 135 over 2026-09-03 to 2026-10-15, a tenfold fall. Both figures include placements now undetermined and excluded from every count since 22 October 2026, in Australia model calls with no checkable marker and uncredited copies of such calls; without them the fall is also tenfold. The tracker counts every placement of a release across a country's monitored outlets over its window, while the original study sampled newspaper pages, so the two Australian figures are not comparable.

            Official Chinese sourcing, verification pending. Not a fifth category. A deterministic pass marks every article that cites Xinhua, CGTN, Global Times, China Daily, CCTV or People's Daily, quotes a foreign ministry spokesperson, a Chinese embassy or a named official spokesperson, or says that Chinese state media reported something. Those are the candidates for unchecked state sourcing. The same line holds the candidates for state origin that the model judges: an article with one weak signature, and one whose headline, summary or body nearly repeats a state origin item another outlet ran with its credit. When more candidates wait than the daily model ceiling allows, the ones sent are a random draw with the same fraction in every country. The eligible and drawn counts are recorded per day as a diagnostic; no count is reweighted by them.

            Gaps in collection. Feeds carry only their latest entries, so a skipped hourly run loses more items in a busy media market than in a quiet one; every poll records whether its window reached back to the previous one, and the estimated missed items are shown. Until 22 October 2026 a relay on the owner's machine collected the outlets that refuse the crawler from its server addresses, and polled many short feeds beside the hosted runner; days on which it ran too few hours stay marked on the timeline, so a sleeping laptop is never read as a decline. Collecting from another network works around a site's choice to refuse the crawler, so the relay was stopped that day: its outlets are inactive since, which leaves the Cook Islands and Kiribati unmonitored and other countries with fewer outlets, as the methodology's known limits list. Ruleset changes and the days still carrying labels from an older ruleset are marked on the timeline too, so a discontinuity is not read as a trend. The relevance gate carries its own version, marked on the timeline as well: it decides what enters the corpus rather than how an article is labelled, and because gate decisions are recorded per item and never revisited, a change to it applies only to items discovered after that date. Items the earlier gate rejected are not re-examined, and rejected items are pruned after three days, except a random 2 percent kept permanently so the gate's recall can be checked by hand for any period.

            Known blind spots. The uncredited copy count is a lower bound. An article is checked as a possible uncredited copy only against state origin items that another monitored outlet ran with a credit line, distribution stamp, sponsored disclosure or diplomat's byline, in the same language and within eleven days either side; a state origin label the model gave naming no evidence is never used for the match, and neither is an undetermined one, such as a sponsored disclosure the model named that the rules did not find: an uncredited copy counts only when the item it repeats is settled state origin, and a copy of an undetermined item is undetermined itself. The English editions of the six state outlets the tracker reads as reference originals are never used for it either. State text that no monitored outlet ever ran with a credit, such as Xinhua copy in Italian, Spanish or Portuguese that every outlet carrying it ran uncredited, cannot be matched, and neither can copy from the state services' editions in other languages, which are not read. Chinese-language outlets outside China are registered but not monitored, under the language rule. That is a blind spot as well as a choice: the original study found state-linked content through this layer, for example in the lifestyle section of a Chinese-language diaspora paper in Canada, so state origin carried in Chinese-language diaspora media is not counted in any figure here.

            The critical distinction is between unchecked state sourcing and independent journalism, and it is a judgement about verification, not about topic or tone. An article highly critical of China that repeats an unverified Xinhua claim is still unchecked state sourcing. An article sympathetic to China that independently confirms every claim is independent journalism. Only a factual claim central to the article counts; a position, policy, warning, threat, opinion or denial attributed to Beijing does not.

            How the tracker differs from the original study. The original study coded 596 articles across four countries: Italy, Canada, Australia and Singapore. The tracker differs from it in four ways, and none of its figures should be set beside the study's without them.

            The unit counted. The original study counted identical wire copy once per publication. The tracker counts every placement in each monitored outlet, so an outlet that runs the same wire copy again, on another day or as a separate article, has two placements where the study counted one. The only exceptions: a story shown by one shared widget across a network's mastheads counts once per country, and one page at two addresses, or the parts of one numbered series, count once. Language editions of one wire release are one underlying item. Underlying items join near copies across all of a country's outlets, so neither number is the study's unit, and the tracker's counts and shares cannot be set beside the study's. The Australian example under Placements and underlying items above shows how far the unit alone moves a figure.

            Not relevant, by the tracker's rule. The study's codebook says a passing mention does not make an article relevant and leaves the line to the coder. Where no signature, official sourcing or near copy sends an article on, the tracker draws that line by its own rule, which is not in the study's codebook: the article concerns China when it has at least three distinct China terms in the body, or five occurrences of them, or a term in the headline with at least two occurrences. Below that it is labelled not relevant without a reading.

            Unchecked state sourcing is looser than the study's. The study scored verification on five levels: corroborated, contested and contextualized count as independent journalism, and merely attributed and unassessed count as unchecked state sourcing, under two fixed rules: a second official or another outlet repeating the claim is not corroboration, and tone does not count. The model is not given the five levels. It is asked whether a central factual claim was "passed on without independent confirmation", and context counts against that only when it concerns the claim itself. An article that sets the claim in independent context without confirming it can therefore land in unchecked state sourcing where the study would code it independent journalism; in the study's Australian sample such contextualized items were 27 of 58. The model's instructions share the rule on tone but not the rule on a second official or outlet, which can move an article the other way. On balance the tracker's unchecked state sourcing probably overcounts relative to the study, and it stays provisional. The five level scale goes into the model's instructions after 23 October 2026, and every unchecked state sourcing label is then judged again.

            Section headers are not evidence here. Much of the study's state origin evidence was a section header, such as a "partnership content from GlobeNewswire" header at a Canadian daily. The tracker's rules find a sponsored or partner disclosure only on the article itself: its own page labels, its byline, its headline, or a line that opens or closes the text; a section header counts only when it names a Chinese state entity. The model sees the headline and text only, never the outlet, the address or the section. Items collected from an outlet's press release, sponsored or partner section are recorded with that arrival, but the arrival alone never makes an item state origin. Paid sections the study would catch can therefore be missed, or end up undetermined: judged state origin by the model alone, which keeps them out of every count. Giving the model the address and the section is planned after 23 October 2026.

              Technical notes

              Operational detail kept as static text in the page's content file, not written from the data; the current figures are in the list below.

              What this can tell you is how often, in a fixed set of outlets that I can actually read, Chinese state text appears without substantive change, and how often local journalists pass on official Chinese factual claims without checking them. What it cannot tell you is what happens behind paywalls I do not cross, in outlets I do not track, in sections I have not searched, in languages I excluded, or in the ordinary gaps of an hourly poll of RSS feeds. A country that looks empty here may simply be a country I have not yet found a working feed for. Read the hatching before you read the color.
