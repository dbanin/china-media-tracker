China state media tracker

This instrument looks for two kinds of article in every country it monitors: text placed by the Chinese state and published unaltered (state origin), and local reporting that carries claims from Chinese officials or state media without independent checking (unverified relay). Ordinary China coverage is only the denominator. Counts come from the full text of articles in a fixed registry of outlets, every day. Hatched countries are not monitored. That is absence of data, not absence of content.

Methodology

State origin. The text was written by an entity of the Chinese state and published essentially unaltered. Xinhua wire copy, CGTN promotional releases, China Daily and Global Times syndication, sponsored or branded placements paid for by Chinese state entities, and signed opinion pieces by Chinese ambassadors and embassy officials. The defining property is that nobody at the receiving publication formed an editorial view about the content.

Unverified relay. The article was written by the local outlet, but it passes on official Chinese sourcing without independent confirmation. A ministry spokesperson is quoted and nothing checks the claim. Xinhua is cited as the authority for a factual assertion with no second source. State media is described as reporting something, and the report is treated as the fact.

Independent journalism. The outlet's own reporting, including reporting that quotes Chinese officials but confirms, contextualizes or contests what they say.

Not relevant. Does not concern China.

Official Chinese sourcing, verification pending. Not a fifth category. A deterministic pass marks every article that cites Xinhua, CGTN, Global Times, China Daily, CCTV or People's Daily, quotes a foreign ministry spokesperson, a Chinese embassy or a named official spokesperson, or says that Chinese state media reported something. Those are the candidates for unverified relay. Whether the article checked the claim is the verification judgement, made by the model stage and audited by hand coding; until it runs, the candidate is shown as a target with the sentence that triggered it.

The critical distinction is between unverified relay and independent journalism, and it is a judgement about verification, not about topic or tone. An article highly critical of China that repeats an unverified Xinhua claim is still unverified relay. An article sympathetic to China that independently confirms every claim is independent journalism.

What this can tell you is how often, in a fixed set of outlets that I can actually read, Chinese state text appears unaltered, and how often local journalists pass on official Chinese claims without checking them. What it cannot tell you is what happens behind paywalls I do not cross, in outlets I do not track, in languages I excluded, or in the ordinary gaps of an hourly poll of RSS feeds. A country that looks empty here may simply be a country I have not yet found a working feed for. Read the hatching before you read the color.
