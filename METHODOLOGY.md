## What the system does

A scheduled job polls the RSS feeds of every active outlet in
sources/outlets.yaml, stores every item before doing anything else, and
applies a loose multilingual keyword gate to the title and summary to decide
whether an item plausibly concerns China. The schedule asks for one run an
hour; GitHub Actions delays and drops scheduled runs under load, so the actual
interval varies and every run's real start and finish are recorded in the run
log. The gate's terms include every Chinese province and the largest cities,
because short wire and release items about one province often name no other
China term. An item whose title or summary already carries a state origin
signature passes the gate whatever its keywords say, so state copy that
announces itself there is not lost for want of a China keyword; state copy
whose only signature sits in the body is still judged on its keywords and can
be rejected. Outlets in Hong Kong,
Macau and Taiwan do not pass the gate on the name of their own territory, and
outlets in Singapore and Malaysia do not pass it on phrases that describe their
own Chinese communities ("Chinese New Year", "Malaysian Chinese Association"),
so local news is not counted as coverage of China.

Some busy outlets are also read through their Google News sitemap, which lists
every recent article with a title and no summary. For those sitemap items only,
the gate also reads a short list of title terms (the title_only block of
pipeline/keywords.yaml): Chinese places, state issuers, trade fairs and
initiatives, and some companies. Where a feed summary would say China, a
sitemap headline often names only the city, the fair or the company, so that
name is the only signal there is. Items from ordinary feeds, which carry a
summary, are never judged on this list. A company is kept on it only where,
among the stored titles it alone would admit, those labelled as China coverage
at least match those labelled not about China; most car, phone, appliance,
solar and drug makers fail that test and are left out. A match is recorded as
"title_only:<term>" among the item's gate terms. On stored PR Newswire titles
the list raised the gate's recall of state origin releases from 103 to 145 of
210, at a cost of about 8 to 25 extra article fetches a day.

The gate has its own version, 2026.10.2, separate from the ruleset: it
decides what enters the corpus, not how an article is labelled. Gate decisions
are recorded per item and never revisited, so a change to the gate applies only
to items discovered after it. Items an earlier gate rejected are not
re-examined, and rejected items are pruned after 3 days, except a
random 2 percent kept permanently for the gate audit. Re-running a
new gate over the rejections still stored measures only what is recoverable
from them; it cannot speak for items already pruned, or for feed entries that
rolled off a feed before any poll saw them, so a small recovery is never
evidence that the earlier gate lost nothing.
The gate last changed on 2026-10-17, to version 2026.10.2.

Where an outlet's press release, sponsored or partner section has been found
(sources/outlets.yaml, release_sections and section_feeds), that section is
polled as well, and its items skip the gate: RSS feeds usually carry editorial
coverage only, while paid placements sit in those sections. At export time 1298 of 1613 active outlets had been searched for such sections: 180 had one, 1016 had none, 11 blocked the search and 91 could not be reached. Where no section was searched, state origin placed through that route cannot be found, and the count per country is part of every result.

Items that pass the gate are fetched in full, honoring robots.txt, at most one
request per domain every three seconds, or less often where the site's
robots.txt sets a longer Crawl-delay (honored up to ten minutes; feed polling is
paced the same way), with an identifying user agent. Pages
that declare themselves not free in schema.org metadata, or that present a
paywall interstitial with a short body, are recorded as paywalled and never
retrieved by any other route. Paywalled articles are never classified, so they
are missing from every count, and from the share of China coverage, where an
unread article is in neither half. They do remain in the share of monitored
output denominator, which counts every item the outlets published whether or
not it could be read, so in a country with many paywalls that share is a lower
bound. A country where paywalls pass a third of retrieved articles carries a
warning on every figure the interface shows for it.

Fetched articles pass through a deterministic signature matcher
(pipeline/signatures.yaml) that detects state origin text by credit lines, datelines,
distribution stamps, sponsored placement disclosures and diplomatic bylines.
Any strong signature, or weak signatures from two different groups, gives
the state origin label with confidence 1.0 and no model call. One weak signature, or any
official sourcing trigger (a citation of Xinhua, CGTN, Global Times, China
Daily, CCTV or People's Daily; a quoted Ministry of Foreign Affairs
spokesperson, Chinese embassy or named official spokesperson; or a
"state media reported" construction in any registry language), routes the
article to a language model for the unchecked state sourcing versus independent
journalism judgement. An article with neither whose headline, feed summary or body
nearly repeats a state origin item another outlet ran from eleven days before it to eleven
days after it goes to the model as a possible copy of state text run without its
credit line (pipeline/lookalike.py), and the model is shown that item's headline and
outlet. Articles with no
signature, no trigger and no such match are labelled independent journalism by rules when
the body substantively concerns China by the tracker's own rule, which is not in the
original study's codebook (Known limits): at least three distinct China terms in the body, or five occurrences of them, or a term in the headline with at least two occurrences,
otherwise not relevant. That threshold applies only to articles no signature
has claimed. Those residual labels carry confidence below 1.0 and are eligible
for the reliability study like every other label.

The gate in front of the model cannot see uncredited relay. Agency copy that a newsroom runs without a credit line names no state outlet, spokesperson or embassy, so it trips no signature and no trigger and, unless another outlet carried the same text as state origin, is labelled by rules, usually as independent journalism, without the model ever reading it; outlets that habitually run wire copy unattributed are where this is most common, and where the counts of state origin and unchecked state sourcing are most likely to be too low. The recall of the gate is measured by hand (pipeline/recall_audit.py): a seeded random sample of the articles the rules settled without a model call, stratified by language and oversampling outlets with a wire copy habit and articles whose headline or summary nearly repeats a state origin item already in the database, is coded by a person who does not see the rule label, and the share placed in state origin or unchecked state sourcing, weighted back to the population, is the gate's miss rate. The miss rate is not yet measured.

The model (claude-sonnet-5) sees the headline and body only. It never sees the
outlet name or the country, so it cannot learn that outlets in particular
countries tend to relay official sourcing. Its prompt carries the codebook's
category definitions, set out more fully than the summaries under The
categories below and with worked examples, and instructs it to answer
independent journalism when
genuinely uncertain, because under-counting unchecked state sourcing is the safer error.
At most 4000 model calls are made a day, and the stage also
keeps to a budget of 110 dollars a calendar month: what is left of

## The categories

State origin. Text authored or commissioned by a Chinese state entity and published without substantive editorial alteration, whatever its topic. State media, government, party and diplomatic bodies, state-owned enterprises and the Hong Kong and Macao governments and statutory bodies are state entities; a press release from any other issuer is not state origin, even when it is published unaltered.

Unchecked state sourcing. Written by the local outlet, and its central content is a factual claim attributed to a Chinese official or state source (an assertion of fact about events, numbers or actions), passed on without independent confirmation. A stated position, policy, warning, threat, opinion or denial is not a factual claim, and reporting one is independent journalism even without outside comment.

Independent journalism. Not state origin and not unchecked state sourcing. It includes the outlet's own reporting; copy from news agencies that are not Chinese state entities, such as Reuters or AP; reporting of a position, policy, warning, threat, opinion or denial attributed to a Chinese official or state source, with or without outside comment; reporting that quotes Chinese officials and confirms, contextualizes or contests what they say; and a press release from an issuer that is not a Chinese state entity, when it substantively concerns China.

Not relevant. Does not concern China in any substantive way. A Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan domestic story counts as China coverage only when it substantively concerns the PRC state or its policy.

The critical distinction is between unchecked state sourcing and independent
journalism, and it is a judgement about verification, not about topic or tone.
An article highly critical of China that repeats an unverified Xinhua claim is
still unchecked state sourcing. An article sympathetic to China that independently
confirms every claim is independent journalism. Only a factual claim central
to the article counts: "Exports rose 8.7 percent in September, China's commerce
ministry said", reported as the figure with no other source, is unchecked state
sourcing, while "China's foreign ministry warned Manila it would take resolute
measures to defend its sovereignty", reported without outside comment, is
independent journalism, because it passes on a position and not a fact the
outlet failed to check. In the database and the data
files these four labels are stored as the codes A, B, C and not_relevant, in
that order, for compactness; the interface never shows the codes. Unchecked
state sourcing is the interface's name for what the codebook and the model
prompt call unverified relay; the prompt keeps that name, and the data files
keep the column name unverified_relay.

## Reliability and what may be published

## Planted bylines

Since 8 October 2026 the pipeline also keeps a watchlist of fictitious journalist and expert personas, and of false front organisations and outlets, that published investigations have named as having placed or pitched articles in real news outlets (sources/personas.yaml, every entry citing its report). Every discovered item, including those the China gate rejected, has its author field compared with the list after names are normalised and initial-less forms are derived; items with a body are also checked for a listed name after a byline word at the top of the text and for a listed front organisation or domain anywhere in the text. A match is recorded as a flag with the detector, the persona or front, the investigation and the matching span, and the item is kept past the usual retention window. A flag means only that the byline or citation matches a name a published investigation has exposed; the tracker cannot prove a byline is fictitious, and the absence of a match proves nothing. Flags never enter the three categories and never change a count. A recurring bylines diagnostic exists on the command line only (python -m pipeline.planted recurring) and is never published, because it lists syndicated agency reporters. At export time the list held 14 personas and 11 fronts from 10 investigations (list version 2026.10.2), and 0 flags had been recorded in all, 0 of them on articles discovered in the last thirty days.

## New measures (provisional)

These measures are report only: none of them changes a label, a count, a denominator or the outlets monitored. Each is marked provisional until its own hand check passes, and withheld if the latest completed hand check fails.

**Alteration score (provisional).** The alteration score tests the second half of the state origin definition, that the text is reproduced without substantive editorial alteration. It sets each state origin copy beside the earliest version of the same text that can be found: the state outlet's own page (a reference original) where one is found, otherwise an earlier copy of the same item at another monitored outlet. Only a reference original is the state text itself; against an earlier monitored copy the score says how far one outlet's copy differs from another's, and every figure says which kind of original it rests on. It is report only: it never changes a label, a count or a denominator, and a copy with a high score keeps its label. At the last export 181 of 2489 state origin copies had an original (66 from a reference source, 115 an earlier monitored copy), and 50 had both texts available to score: 20 against a reference original and 30 against an earlier monitored copy, from 33 distinct originals and 33 distinct underlying items.

An original is looked for first among the reference sources, the English and other editions of Chinese state outlets read only as originals. A reference item qualifies when the copy's credit names its publisher, it is in the copy's language, it appeared from eleven days before the copy to one day after it, and its headline, feed summary or body fingerprint nearly repeats the copy's; when both texts are available at least 30 percent of the copy's words must line up with it. Failing that, the original is the earliest credited copy of the same item at another monitored outlet, in the same language, outside the copy's own media group: sister papers of one group (the ACM regional mastheads, the Postmedia papers, the Nine or News Corp mastheads) share one copy pool, so a sister's earlier copy is the same placement, not an original. Translations are never matched across languages, so an Italian copy of Xinhua English has no reference original and is counted as a translation. Copies first seen in the last 14 days are paired again at every export.

Both texts are compared after credit lines, datelines and lines an outlet repeats on many of its pages are removed. The score is the share of the copy's words that are not found in the original, in runs of three words or more. Cuts are reported separately as the share of the original kept, and headline changes as a headline similarity. The provisional bands are verbatim (under 3 percent new words), light (under 15 percent), moderate (under 40 percent) and substantial (40 percent or more). Many copies repeat one original, so the shares in the lead count each underlying item once, with the number of copies beside them, and an outlet is ranked, or a median given, only from 5 distinct originals.

The bands measure wording, not meaning. A copy can keep every word and still change the sense by cutting a paragraph, and a copy can score as substantial because an outlet added its own background or a rebuttal. Two keyword cues, one for local context and one for counterpoint, flag added text for a reader to check; they are weak and never a finding on their own. Bodies are stored only where the tracker runs, so only copies whose two texts are both available are scored, and the coverage line says how many that is; each reference source's counts of pages fetched, expired, failed and paywalled are given beside it. Every copy in the substantial band is listed for review by hand, and a blind hand check of 20 pairs per band tests both the pairs and the bands.

**Republication speed (provisional).** Republication speed is the time between a state original and a state origin copy of it in a monitored outlet. It is measured only for copies paired with their original (see the alteration score), and only when both publication times are credible: readable, not after the item was discovered, and not before collection began. The time the tracker discovered an item is never used as a lag. Where the original is the state outlet's own page, collected as a reference source, the lag is absolute. Where no such page was found and the original is the first monitored copy, the lag is relative, behind the first monitored copy, and the two are never pooled. Lags fall into four buckets: the same hour, the same day (1 to 24 hours), 1 to 3 days, and later. A copy up to an hour ahead of its original counts as the same hour, since clocks differ by minutes. When either page gives only a date, the pair is bucketed by calendar date instead (same date, 1 to 3 days, later) and kept out of the medians. The days are counted in one zone, the zone of the date: the state outlets' dates are Beijing dates (the Global Times sitemap's are UTC dates), and the other page's time is converted into that zone before the days are counted. Copies more than an hour ahead of their original, and pairs without a credible time, are counted but kept outside every distribution. One state text run by many outlets of one group is many pairs but one original, so every figure gives the distinct originals beside the pairs, and a median needs distinct originals, not pairs: an outlet gets a median from 10 timed pairs with 10 distinct originals; a route, country or source from 5. An earlier copy at a sister outlet of the same media group is never the first monitored copy. Wires are counted under WLD. At the last export, 53 absolute pairs fell inside the distributions, with 41 distinct originals.

The times are what the pages say, and they are not always what they seem. Many sites give the time a page was last updated as its publication time: on the copy that makes it look slower than it was, and on the original it can put a copy ahead of its source. Some state listings label their time zones wrongly: a Beijing time written as UTC reads eight hours late. Times are corrected per source when they are collected, and the export checks every reference source for this by comparing publication with discovery: the median gap, the share published after it was discovered, and the share that give only a date, overall and among items more than a day old, by when the page's own time should have been read. Discovery can only show a time that reads late. A time that reads early is caught where a source lists an item twice: CGTN's sitemap writes Beijing time marked as UTC, so its times are read as Beijing time, and every item it shares with CGTN's RSS feed is compared; if CGTN began writing true UTC, the sitemap would read eight hours earlier than the feed and the source would be flagged. Each source's counts of pages fetched, expired, failed and paywalled are given beside it. Pages that give only a date can say no more than the calendar day. State text also reaches outlets by channels the tracker cannot see, such as wire terminals, email and agreements, so a lag is the time between two public pages, not the time it took the text to travel. Coverage is partial: only copies paired with an original count, the reference sources reach back only a few days, and most copies have no original found. The hand check compares the bucket of 60 reference pairs, read by a person from the times shown on both pages, with the measure's bucket, and passes at 85 percent agreement.

**Doctrine terms (provisional).** The doctrine term tracker counts how often China coverage uses the Chinese state's own set phrases, such as community with a shared future, true multilateralism, Chinese modernization and the one-China principle. There are 13 families of terms. Each family is matched in English and in the renderings Chinese state media and the Chinese foreign ministry publish in the outlet's language; a language is left out of a family when no such rendering was found, or when the rendering is ordinary prose in that language, and every language is also matched on the English terms. The terms are read in the headline, the feed summary and the whole body, and every occurrence is counted, though an acronym in brackets beside its full term is the same mention and counts once with it. A match is a keyword match, not a reading of the article: it says the phrase is there, not who used it or whether the article endorses it.

Some matches are excluded by rule. A bare win-win is ordinary English and never counts, only win-win cooperation; hegemonism counts but the generic hegemony does not; the one-China principle is kept apart from the one China policy other governments state. An acronym counts only when a full term of the same family appears in the same article, and GSI only beside Global Security Initiative itself, so BRI in Indonesian football or banking news is not the Belt and Road. Belt and Road, Multipolar world, Global South and People-to-people exchanges are common well beyond Chinese state usage, so they are shown muted and left out of the share of articles using the vocabulary. Multipolar world and People-to-people exchanges are also the stated policy of other governments, India, Russia, Vietnam and the BRICS among them, so they are counted only when a China word (China, Chinese, Beijing, Xi Jinping and their renderings in the article's language) appears within 140 characters of the phrase.

Rates are per 1,000 China articles of each kind (state origin, unchecked state sourcing, independent journalism) on the same days, counting only articles already tagged under the current term list. Where a body has not been read the headline and summary alone are matched, which finds fewer phrases; the article is tagged again once its body arrives. A hand check codes up to 200 matches, spread evenly over the families first and over each family's languages within it, each shown with about 30 words either side, stored when the article is tagged so the sample does not depend on where it is drawn. A family is checked at a precision of at least 0.9 on at least 15 coded matches and withheld below 0.8 once 5 are coded, and a withheld family shows no figure; the measure as a whole is checked when the matches of the families still shown reach 0.9 together.

**Unusual days (provisional).** Unusual days are days on which a country, or the worldwide press release wires, carried far more state origin text than usual. What is counted is the day's distinct underlying items, each outlet adding at most 3 of them, so one outlet's batch or one item placed many times is a lump and never a surge on its own. Each complete day is compared with the 28 complete days before it, counting only the days on which the country's outlets were collected at all, and at least 14 of them are needed. The expected count is the mean of those days after the busiest tenth of them is set aside, and never less than 0.2. Daily counts vary more than chance alone would make them, so the spread is taken from the country's own last 90 days (a negative binomial model). A day is flagged when it counts at least 5, at least three times the expected count, with a chance under 1 in 1,000 of reaching that count on an ordinary day. The day the export runs is partial and is never tested. China is not monitored and has no series. Over the 8,942 country days tested so far, 3 were flagged. The model puts the number it would flag by chance alone at about 0.1, but it assumes each country's days vary as they did before, and real days are lumpier, so that figure is a rough guide and not a promise.

Each flagged day lists the outlets and underlying items behind it, and the checks that could make it an artefact of the instrument rather than a surge of state text: a newly added outlet, a backfill read, a collector recovering from an outage, an incomplete day for the relay on the owner's machine (stopped on 22 October 2026), a ruleset or relevance gate change, saturated feeds on the baseline days, the daily model cap, and a surge in everything discovered rather than in state text alone. A day with any of these is shown muted with its reason, as one to check, and a day with none is shown as clean. A ruleset change counts only when it took effect inside the 28 days a day is compared with and the labels on the two sides still differ in age: until the older days are labelled again under the new rules, those days stay to check, and the site says so; a change stops affecting new days 28 days after it. The repeats in the discovery counts before they were fixed are noted too, but they touch only the rate per discovered item, not the count. Unusual days are report only: they change no count, and a hand check of up to 30 flagged days, each judged a real surge or an artefact, has to find at least 80 percent real before the marking provisional comes off.

Each flagged day also says what it was about, from the tracker's own data and in counts only: the themes and the terms of its state origin items and of the day before's, and of the country's other China coverage that day, a term being listed only when at least 2 articles carry it and it is at least twice as common as on the 28 days before, so the words every day carries drop out. Beside it, where one was found, is an event in the news that coincides with the day, researched by hand from a major outlet or an official site and recorded with its source (sources/spike_context.yaml), or a note that no matching event was found. Such a note shows timing, not cause: a day from one wire feed often follows a single event that wire covered, and the checks above, listed beside the day, can explain it on their own.

## What the numbers mean

The default measure is state origin only. It is not a label decided without
the model: at export time fixed rules decided 972 of the 1,109 state origin labels (88 percent) from a signature, and 137 (12 percent) are model judgements that name a credit line, distribution stamp or diplomat's byline, or match a credited item. A further 1,380 model judgements of state origin name no evidence a reader can check, name a sponsored disclosure the rules did not find, or repeat an undetermined item; they are undetermined, excluded from every count, and in none of these numbers (see Undetermined items). Model judgements are not perfectly repeatable, and hand coding checks them like every other model label. There is no combined state-linked measure: adding the
candidates still waiting for the verification judgement to judged counts would
mix a measured quantity with an unmeasured pile whose composition varies by
country. The waiting candidates are shown only as their own figure, in the
country panel and the CSV exports, and are not a finding.

Every figure counts an item on the UTC day it was discovered, the only date the instrument observes. The exception is an item brought in by a backfill read (the AAP release reader's catch-up reads, a feed's first poll, and its first poll after more than 3 days without one) and discovered more than 36 hours after it was published, which counts on its publication day if that is on or after 2026-09-03, in the counts and in the share of monitored output denominator alike, because a backfill would otherwise put weeks of items on one day. A backfilled item on a day before a gate change marked on the timeline was admitted by the gate in force when it was read (version 2026.10.2 at this export), not by the earlier gate that applied to the other items of that day.

Raw counts mostly measure how many outlets the registry happens to track in a
country. Articles per monitored outlet corrects for registry density. The share
of monitored output divides by every item the country's monitored outlets put
in their feeds in the window, whether or not it concerns China;
the largest outlets are the 30 best audience ranks recorded in
sources/outlets.yaml, and 214 countries carry ranks so far and every other country uses all of its active outlets. A share of published items is not
shown for a country with fewer than 5 outlets behind it, where it
would describe a handful of feeds rather than a country. No per capita figure
is shown, because population is a poor denominator for what outlets publish.
The resident population of each country is still exported, in the population
column of the CSV of the current view, and no figure is divided by it.

A syndicated wire item placed in thirty outlets and thirty separate items are
different phenomena. Near-duplicate titles within a country are linked as one
underlying item; the interface shows both the number of placements and the
number of underlying items. An underlying item counts once in a window
however many of its days it was placed on, and once in a world figure however
many countries carried it. It counts under each label it carries, so an item
with a state origin placement and an unchecked state sourcing placement is in
both counts.

Counts are sensitive to how duplicates are defined. The rule used here
(pipeline/placements.py) counts a release shown by one shared widget across a
network of mastheads once per country, like a release on a distribution wire.
Counting each release that one shared AAP widget showed across ten ACM
mastheads once per country, instead of once per masthead, cut Australia's
state origin from 1,350 placements to 135 over 2026-09-03 to 2026-10-15, a
tenfold fall. Both figures include placements now undetermined, excluded from
every count since 22 October 2026 (all of them, in Australia, model calls with
no checkable marker); without them the fall is also tenfold. The tracker counts every placement of a release across a
country's monitored outlets over its window, while the original study sampled
newspaper pages, so the tracker's figure for Australia and the study's are not
comparable.

Titles are the first pass. An outlet often rewrites a wire headline, so a second pass compares the fetched text. Every body is cut into overlapping 5 word shingles (5 character shingles in scripts written without spaces) and summarised as a 64 value MinHash signature. Two articles from different outlets, in any countries, discovered within 7 days of each other, are linked as one underlying item when their signatures estimate that at least 50 percent of their shingles are shared. Pairs from the same outlet are left to title matching, and a body that matches another article of its own outlet under a different headline is treated as page template text and never linked. Measured on 6,855 stored bodies on 2026-10-12, the body pass linked 124 pairs of articles that title matching had left apart, 104 of them across countries, and none of a hand checked sample of 30 of those pairs joined two different items (one joined two successive versions of one wire story, and one a newspaper's rewrite of a news agency's copy); below the threshold, pairs began to join different versions or different stories about one event. Translations are not matched: a translated copy shares almost no words with its original, and matching it would need machine translation, which the tracker does not do. Body links change no category count, and the model's label is copied only within a title group, never along a body link. Body links can only lower the number of underlying items, and only where the linked placements fall in the same figure and the same category: in a country's figure when both placements are in that country, and in a world figure wherever they are. A link across countries marks both placements as syndicated and leaves each country's own figure as it was. Underlying items are counted once per window, so a story placed on several days of a window is one item, not one per day.

Global press release distribution services (us_businesswire, us_globenewswire, us_prnewswire) are not national
newsrooms: their releases are written by their issuers and pushed to
subscribers in many countries at once. They are polled and classified like any
outlet, and they are counted as a stratum of their own, "Worldwide
distribution", under the code WLD in the data files. The stratum appears in
the ranked list, the country search and the CSV exports beside the countries,
and nowhere on the map, because it has no territory to draw. It is never
attributed to the United States, where the services are registered, and never
added into any individual country's numbers: a release is not tied to any one
country, and adding it to each country it might reach would count it once per
country. World totals include it. Its share of monitored output divides by
everything the wires themselves published. At export time the stratum held 1375 gated articles: 98 state origin, 2 unchecked state sourcing, 90 independent journalism, 740 not relevant and 9 awaiting the verification judgement. A further 307 are undetermined, model calls of state origin whose authorship the evidence does not settle, excluded from every count. A Distribution toggle beside the Measure control shows
the two apart: Both is the whole dataset, Country outlets leaves the wires out
of every figure that sums across countries, and Worldwide wires shows the
stratum alone, with the map left unfilled because a release is not tied to
any one country; the count of outlets monitored and the leaning chart count
newsrooms only and never include the wires in any mode.

The color scale is fixed for a measure and window across every date on the
timeline: the darkest step starts at the 95th percentile of the values of all
dates, not of the day shown, so a color means the same number on every date and
the countries above the cap sit in an overflow step. The cap holds only until
the next data update. Each update, about once a day, adds the newest days and
recomputes the cap over the timeline as it then stands, so a color can stand
for a different number from one week to the next. The theme counter's cap
works the same way. All China coverage is the exception: it runs from a handful
of articles to thousands per country, so its seven steps split the values of
every date whose window is complete into about equal shares instead of rising to
a cap, and still mean the same number on every date until the next update.

Absence of data and absence of content are different things. A country with no
monitored outlets is drawn with a hatch pattern. A country whose outlets all
publish in a language without a keyword list is drawn as unreadable, because
the gate can only match international and English terms there. A country with
monitored outlets and no detections is drawn flat. Only detections above zero
use the color scale. A country whose feeds are mostly failing, whose articles
are mostly paywalled, whose feeds lose items between polls, or whose relayed
outlets were not collected carries a warning that travels with every figure
for it, in the tooltip, the ranked list, the country panel and the CSV export.

Feeds carry only their most recent entries. When a poll returns a full window
with nothing seen before, items published since the previous successful poll
were probably missed; a skipped run costs a busy media market more than a quiet
one. Every poll records whether that happened, and the estimated number of
missed items is the feed's own publishing rate times the gap. The rate is taken
over at least two hours, or over the feed's median window span in the last
three days when that is shorter (never under a quarter of an hour), so a window
stamped within minutes cannot imply hundreds of items an hour. One poll's
estimate is at most twice the new items the feed delivered per day over the
previous fourteen days, scaled up for a gap longer than a day. A feed polled at
least three times a day that delivers no more than two and a half windows a
day publishes in batches: a poll of it whose entries are stamped within an
hour of one another counts only the batches that fell wholly between it and the
previous successful poll, none when that poll came less than one batch
interval earlier. Until 2026-10-12 the rate was taken over the window alone
and five such batch feeds were booked about 15,000 phantom misses. Each day's
poll record names the estimator that made it, and the older estimates cannot be
made again because single poll times were not kept, so the published estimate
counts only the polls made by the current estimator, from the day it was first
used, while polls and full windows count every poll on record. Until
2026-10-22 a relay collector on the owner's machine also collected the
outlets that refuse the runner, and polled many short feeds beside it. It
recorded every hourly pass; a day on which it ran in fewer than
12 hours is marked incomplete for the countries whose outlets
only it collected, so a sleeping laptop never reads as a decline. The outlets
it shared with the hosted runner are not marked: the runner polled their feeds
on its own schedule and fetched their pages throughout. The relay was stopped
on 2026-10-22, and its outlets are inactive since (Known limits); no
day after it is marked, and the shared outlets are polled by the hosted runner
alone.

Themes. Every article that concerns China is tagged with each theme whose
terms appear in its headline, its feed summary or anywhere in its body
(pipeline/themes.yaml, themes version 2026.09.4): diplomacy and summits,
trade and economy, Belt and Road and investment, technology, energy and
electric vehicles, military and security, Taiwan and Hong Kong, human rights,
Chinese domestic politics, culture and tourism, and disasters and health.
Until themes version 2026.09.4 only the first 800 characters of the body were
read, which favored whatever an article names early.
Themes versions, each with the day it took effect: 2026.09.3 from 2026-09-14 (theme counter introduced; terms read in the title, the feed summary and the first 800 characters of the body); 2026.09.4 from 2026-09-15 (terms read anywhere in the body instead of the first 800 characters; every article re-tagged). Every export re-tags each relevant article whose tags were made under another version, so every article carries the tags of the current version and the published series has no break at a version change; the marks on the theme timeline record when the method changed, and a theme count read or downloaded before a mark was made under the earlier version. An article can carry
several themes, so theme counts do not add up to article counts; one with none
is Other. Theme terms exist for ar, de, en, es, fa, fr, hi, id, it, ja, ko, ms, nl, pl, pt, ro, ru, th, tr, vi; articles in other languages
are matched on the English terms only. The tags are keyword matches, not a
reading of the article, and should be checked by hand before a theme count is
cited.

## Known limits

What the instrument cannot see, beyond the collection gaps described above.

Chinese-language outlets are excluded. Outlets that publish primarily in Chinese are registered and kept inactive, because the owner does not read Chinese and machine translation would miss the distinctions the coding turns on. 19 outlets are excluded this way: 7 in Australia, 5 in Taiwan, 3 in Hong Kong, 2 in Malaysia, 1 in Singapore and 1 in South Korea. A country's figures describe only its outlets in other languages, so where much of its press publishes in Chinese, as in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore and Malaysia, a low count or a zero is not a finding about that press. The exclusion is a blind spot as well as a choice. Chinese-language outlets outside China are registered but not monitored, and the original study found state-linked content through exactly this layer, for example in the lifestyle section of a Chinese-language diaspora paper in Canada. State origin carried in Chinese-language diaspora media is therefore not counted in any figure here.

Session labels. 5,869 of the 16,046 current model labels (37 percent) were made in an interactive session on the owner's machine rather than through the API, each naming in its model version the model that made it. The session reads text fetched again on that machine, not the runner's copy, so it can read a different version of a page, and some pages it cannot fetch at all. In a check against the API on 2026-09-30 (data/labels/README.md) the two agreed on 78 of 98 articles; of the 28 the API called unchecked state sourcing the session called 12 independent journalism, and the reverse happened twice. Session labels therefore undercount unchecked state sourcing relative to the API, and the published figure is lower than an all API run would give. That check was made with the classifier model itself; session labels made by another model are not covered by it.

Translated state copy run without a credit is invisible. The lookalike check and the body pass compare words, and a translation shares almost none with its original, so a state item translated and run with no credit line is matched to nothing. Unless it cites an official Chinese source, which sends it to the model, it is labelled by rules, usually as independent journalism.

The uncredited copy count is a lower bound. An article is sent to the model as a possible uncredited copy only when it nearly repeats a state origin item that another monitored outlet ran, from 11 days before it to 11 days after it, under evidence a credit supports: a wire credit or dateline, a distribution stamp, a sponsored disclosure, a diplomat's byline, or a rules label from before routes were stored. A state origin label that rests on the model's judgement alone is never such a reference, and neither is an undetermined one, such as a sponsored disclosure the model named that the rules did not find: an uncredited copy counts only when the item it repeats is settled state origin, and a copy of an undetermined item is undetermined itself. Headlines are compared only within one language, and feed summaries and bodies by the words they share, so the original has to be in the article's language. The English editions of Xinhua, CGTN, Global Times, People's Daily, China Daily and ECNS that the tracker reads as reference originals are used only to set credited copies beside the text they came from; they are never a reference for this match. The check can therefore find state text only where some monitored outlet also ran it with a credit, in the same language, in that window. It cannot find state text that no monitored outlet ever ran with a credit, such as Xinhua copy in Italian, Spanish or Portuguese that every outlet carrying it ran uncredited, and it cannot find copy from the state services' editions in other languages, which the tracker does not read. The model then decides whether each match is that state text without substantive alteration.

Outlets that refuse the crawler from its server addresses are not collected. Some sites answer the GitHub Actions runner's addresses with an HTTP 403 or a time out while their feeds work from an ordinary network. From September 2026 until 2026-10-22 such outlets were collected from the owner's home connection and passed to the runner (the relay). Collecting from another network works around a site's choice to refuse the crawler, which sits badly with a crawler whose identity never changes and with never bypassing a paywall or an access control, so the relay was stopped on 2026-10-22 and the outlets it collected are inactive since. Their articles collected before then stay in every figure and in the audit files; days on which the relay ran too few hours stay marked up to that day. That left 123 outlets inactive in 89 countries. Every monitored outlet was one of them in Cook Islands (1) and Kiribati (1), so they are no longer monitored from 2026-10-22. 87 countries keep other outlets but lose some, given as outlets dropped of those that were active: Albania 1 of 5, Australia 1 of 30, Austria 1 of 7, Azerbaijan 2 of 8, Belarus 1 of 3, Belize 1 of 5, Benin 1 of 6, Bolivia 1 of 5, British Virgin Islands 1 of 2, Bulgaria 1 of 7, Cambodia 1 of 9, Canada 2 of 25, Central African Republic 1 of 3, Colombia 1 of 10, Costa Rica 1 of 10, Cuba 1 of 12, Curaçao 1 of 4, Côte d'Ivoire 1 of 6, Democratic Republic of the Congo 1 of 10, Fiji 1 of 3, Gabon 1 of 6, Ghana 1 of 8, Guam 1 of 3, Honduras 1 of 7, Iceland 1 of 7, India 1 of 29, Indonesia 3 of 13, Iraq 1 of 6, Israel 4 of 20, Italy 2 of 23, Jamaica 1 of 5, Kazakhstan 1 of 8, Kenya 1 of 10, Lebanon 3 of 8, Lesotho 1 of 6, Lithuania 1 of 10, Macau 1 of 4, Malawi 1 of 9, Malaysia 4 of 12, Mali 1 of 7, Mauritius 2 of 5, Mexico 1 of 14, Montserrat 1 of 2, Morocco 2 of 11, Mozambique 1 of 10, Myanmar 2 of 7, Nepal 2 of 8, Niger 1 of 8, Nigeria 2 of 18, North Korea 1 of 3, North Macedonia 1 of 11, Oman 1 of 5, Pakistan 1 of 12, Palestine 3 of 6, Papua New Guinea 1 of 5, Peru 1 of 12, Philippines 1 of 9, Poland 1 of 15, Romania 1 of 14, Réunion 2 of 5, Saint Kitts and Nevis 1 of 6, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines 1 of 5, Saudi Arabia 1 of 6, Senegal 1 of 11, Slovakia 1 of 9, Slovenia 1 of 6, Solomon Islands 1 of 6, South Sudan 1 of 5, Spain 1 of 15, Sri Lanka 4 of 9, Suriname 1 of 9, Syria 3 of 13, Taiwan 1 of 5, Tajikistan 1 of 5, Thailand 2 of 13, Tonga 1 of 4, Trinidad and Tobago 1 of 4, Tunisia 1 of 15, U.S. Virgin Islands 1 of 3, Uganda 1 of 6, Ukraine 3 of 16, United Kingdom 1 of 15, United States 3 of 44, Uruguay 2 of 8, Venezuela 3 of 13, Yemen 1 of 10 and Zambia 1 of 6. Their counts from 2026-10-22 cover the remaining outlets only, so a fall at that date is the gap, not a change in coverage of China.

Licensed archive check. Paywalled articles are missing from every count. To look behind some paywalls without crossing them, the owner ran the tracker's state origin signatures and model triggers (pipeline/signatures.yaml) as a rules only screen in ProQuest TDM Studio (licensed full text, non-consumptive), editorial pages only, September to October 2026, for 7 titles in Canada, Singapore and Argentina (sources/archive_checks.yaml). No model and no person read the articles, and only aggregate counts left the archive. Of the 1,597 records left after removing duplicates (1,613 pulled), 380 concern China and 1,217 do not; none carried a state origin signature. Of the China articles, 59 cite Chinese official or state sources and await review inside the archive, because their text cannot reach the tracker's model; the other 321 are recorded as no state signature found, which is not independent journalism, because no verification step ran. Valor Econômico returned no records: it was not searchable in the database, which says nothing about what it carried. These counts come from a different source with a different method, so they are not part of any total, map, share, top 10 or finding.

The outlets are not a sample of each country's press. They were found by probing candidate feed addresses and kept only where a feed returned items, so outlets without a working feed are missing, and 365 registered outlets are inactive because they refuse the identified crawler (an HTTP 403 or a robots.txt rule) and 31 more because every feed failed. Raw counts therefore partly measure which outlets publish feeds and admit crawlers, which is one reason the per outlet and share bases exist.

Long articles are cut for the model. The model reads at most the first 12,000 characters of a body (config.LLM_BODY_CHAR_LIMIT); the rest is cut off and the cut is marked in the text it is given. The signature rules look for credit lines at the head and the foot of every body whatever its length, but a factual claim, or the check of one, that comes only after the cut is not seen by the model.

Who counts as the Chinese state. Commercial releases of Chinese state-owned enterprises (GAC, CRRC, Sinopec, State Grid and others) count as state origin, and so do releases and placements of the Hong Kong and Macao governments and their statutory bodies (HKSAR government departments, the Hong Kong Trade Development Council, the Hong Kong Tourism Board, the Macao government); both carry an issuer type, state_owned_enterprise or sar_government, so they can be counted apart by a reader who draws the line elsewhere. A release counts as state origin only when a Chinese state entity issued or commissioned it: releases from private Chinese companies, Hong Kong or Macao private bodies, universities, charities, trade fair organisers and foreign organisations are not state origin however unaltered they run, and are independent journalism when they substantively concern China and not relevant otherwise. A Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan domestic story with no mainland angle counts as China coverage only when it substantively concerns the Chinese state or its policy.

How the tracker differs from the original study. The original study coded 596 articles across four countries: Italy, Canada, Australia and Singapore. The tracker applies its categories continuously but differs from it in four ways, and none of its figures should be set beside the study's without them.

The unit counted. The original study counted identical wire copy once per publication. The tracker counts every placement in each monitored outlet, so an outlet that runs the same wire copy again, on another day or as a separate article, has two placements where the study counted one. The only exceptions are those of pipeline/placements.py: a story shown by one shared widget across a network's mastheads counts once per country, and one page at two addresses, or the parts of one numbered series at one outlet on one day, count once. Language editions of one wire release stay separate placements and are one underlying item. Underlying items join near copies across all of a country's outlets, so neither the tracker's placements nor its underlying items are the study's unit, and the tracker's counts and shares cannot be set beside the study's. The Australian example under What the numbers mean shows how far the unit alone moves a figure.

Not relevant, by the tracker's rule. The study's codebook says that a passing mention does not make an article relevant and leaves the line to the coder. Where no signature, official sourcing trigger or lookalike match sends an article on, the tracker draws that line by its own rule, which is not in the study's codebook: the article concerns China when it has at least three distinct China terms in the body, or five occurrences of them, or a term in the headline with at least two occurrences (pipeline/classify_rules.py, body_relevance). The same rule decides whether an item from a press release, sponsored or partner section with one weak signature goes to the model. An article below it is labelled not relevant by rules, without a reading.

Unchecked state sourcing is looser than the study's. The study scored verification on five levels: corroborated, contested and contextualized count as independent journalism, and merely attributed and unassessed count as unchecked state sourcing, under two fixed rules: a second official or another outlet repeating the claim is not corroboration, and tone does not count. The model is not given the five levels. It is asked whether a central factual claim was "passed on without independent confirmation", and context counts against that only when it concerns the claim itself: background that takes the claim as given, such as details of a vessel or a company or analysts discussing what a figure means, does not. An article that sets the claim in independent context without confirming it can therefore land in unchecked state sourcing where the study would code it independent journalism; in the study's Australian sample such contextualized items were 27 of 58. The model's instructions share the rule on tone, but they do not say that a second official or another outlet repeating the claim is not confirmation, which can move an article the other way. On balance the tracker's unchecked state sourcing probably overcounts relative to the study, and it stays provisional. The five level scale goes into the model's instructions after 23 October 2026, and every unchecked state sourcing label is then judged again.

Section headers are not evidence here. Much of the study's state origin evidence was a section header, such as a "partnership content from GlobeNewswire" header at a Canadian daily. The tracker's rules find a sponsored or partner disclosure only on the article itself: its own page labels, its author field, its headline, or a line that opens or closes the body; a line of the page around it, such as a section header, counts only when that line names a Chinese state entity. The model sees the headline and body only, never the outlet, the address or the section. Items collected from an outlet's press release, sponsored or partner section are recorded with that arrival, on each article and per day and country, but the arrival alone never makes an item state origin. Paid sections the study would catch can therefore be missed, or end up undetermined: judged state origin by the model alone, with no evidence it could name, which keeps them out of every count. Giving the model the address and the section is planned after 23 October 2026.

## Reproducibility

Every classification row records the ruleset version and, for model labels,
the model version. When the signature list changes, the ruleset version is
bumped in pipeline/config.py, the change is recorded in CHANGELOG.md, and every
earlier article is reclassified under the new ruleset during the hourly runs,
not only new ones. Until that pass finishes, the timeline marks each ruleset
change and each day that still carries labels from an older version, so a
discontinuity is never read as a trend. Earlier rows are kept and marked not
current, so a chart made from the database at any date can be reproduced.
Human review labels are stored in a separate table and never overwrite
machine labels.

The database itself is not committed to git, because a growing SQLite file
exceeds GitHub's file size limit within weeks. It travels between runs
through the GitHub Actions cache, together with the extracted article
bodies. Every day a compressed copy is published as an asset of the public
db-snapshot release, scrubbed of bylines, feed summaries, raw model output,
reviewer names and email addresses (pipeline/snapshot_scrub.py). The whole
database and the article bodies are never uploaded, in clear or encrypted,
because article text in a public release is redistribution whether or not it
is sealed. Raw HTML
is kept for the duration of a run and on any machine that runs the fetch
stage locally; the page chrome labels the sponsored placement patterns need
are extracted at fetch time and stored with the article, so they survive
without the HTML. The permanent record in git is data/export, one JSON line per
gated article per month with its current classification, route and arrival,
plus the site data under docs/data. One deliberate exception to the never-delete
rule: items the relevance gate rejected are pruned after 3 days. They
carry no classification. A random 2 percent of them is kept for
audit, permanently: the choice is made by a hash of the item's address, so it is
reproducible and does not depend on outlet, country or language, and each kept
item keeps its title, summary, address, outlet, country, language and the gate
terms it was judged on. pipeline/gate_audit.py draws its hand-checked sample from
that set, stratified by language, over any period, so the gate's recall can be
measured for months already past and not only for the last 3 days.
The address of every pruned item is remembered, for 120 days after a
feed last listed it, or 14 days after its discovery when no feed
lists it again, so a feed that keeps an item listed for weeks does not bring it
in, and count it among the items screened, a second time. Before
2026-10-14 it did: after every prune such an item came back as new. From
2026-10-01 to 2026-10-11, 8.2 percent of the items screened
were such repeats, from almost nothing in Serbia and Croatia to half in Sweden
and nearly three quarters in the Cayman Islands. Since collection began at
least 4.1 percent were (123,682 items the stored
database snapshots show coming back); applying each outlet's share in those
eleven days to the whole period puts it near 8 percent of
the 3,008,671 items screened when it was measured, about 6 percent of the running total of 4,273,298 items screened at this export, and the site says so under that total.
The counts before 2026-10-14 are left as they were: the addresses that
would show which items came back were pruned with the items, so they cannot be
corrected item by item, and a correction of only the days the snapshots cover
would put a step into every series. Each country's share of its own output (%
of all its news) divides by these counts, so over days before
2026-10-14 it reads low by that country's share of repeats, which is
uneven: of the items each country screened from 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-11,
about 16 percent in Britain, 18 in Vietnam, 28 in Uruguay, 47 in Kuwait, 50
in Sweden and 72 in the Cayman Islands were repeats. The map's key says so for
any window that reaches back before 2026-10-14.
